# Session — 2026-08-16/17: buttresses, a walker that could not climb, and battlements

*One conversation (several compactions) spanning the 16th into the
17th. Three arcs: the wall-detailing build order reached **buttresses**
(face piers and clasping corner blocks) and then **crenellations**; a
rendering bug on the buttress turned out to be a missing tangent
stream on every runtime mesh in the project; and a player report that
stairs had stopped being climbable took four rounds of wrong theories
before a per-frame trace found it in one attempt.*

## Goal

Continue wall detailing along the order agreed earlier (string course →
quoins → **buttresses** → **crenellations**), and fix whatever the
user's hand-testing surfaced along the way. Both remaining items
shipped.

## What we did

**1. Buttresses** (`WallButtress.cs`, new). A face pier is EDGE data
(`WallEdge.Buttress` — parameter, side, absolute top) and a clasping
corner pier is NODE data (`WallNode.Buttress` — bearing, absolute
top), because a block belonging to two walls at once belongs to
neither and nodes have always owned joints here. `WallButtress` is the
render part only and serves both owners (exactly one of `edge`/`node`
set). The Buttress tool (own bar button) points at a face and clicks;
**Ctrl+scroll dials the TOP** as a drop below the wall's own top, so it
carries meaningfully from wall to wall. Two refusals with reasons: an
end is not a joint (`MasonryContinuesPast`), and crowding is asked of
the WORLD rather than the edge, since the neighbour a foot away
usually belongs to the next edge along. `CarryOpenings` became
`CarryDetails`, carrying both kinds of edge data in one pass.
CastleSave v10 (edge piers) then v11 (corner piers).

**THE FOUNDING RULE** (the user's call, and the reason no face
restriction was needed): *a pier rests on the first solid thing
beneath it and runs continuously up from there.* Two cases, and they
are not one clamp — a level platform under the WHOLE footprint carries
it (every corner tested by `SlabTile.Contains`), otherwise it goes to
the earth, buried below the lowest of its own four plan corners.
`Ground.HeightAt` cannot see slabs, which is why a `Max` against
`FloorY` got the platform case right only by accident and the rim case
wrong, and why the `Min` that fixed the rim then plunged the platform
case through its own floor. **The user caught that inversion**;
asking what is actually underneath gets both right for the same
reason. Machine-verified on Grosmont: of 224 face placements on
slab-borne walls, 90 rest on a platform and 134 reach the earth — the
two faces of the same wall giving different answers.

The clasping corner block runs the SAME arithmetic as the wedge mesh
on the cursor's own bearing from the node, so standing on the outer
face of an L gets a block while the inner face falls in a gap with no
exposed arc and drops through to an ordinary face pier. At 180° it
degenerates EXACTLY to a face pier with no special case anywhere.

**2. The tangent rule** — the session's most cross-cutting finding.
The user reported "one of the buttress side faces is see through, it
changes sides depending on what angle I look from". Diagnosed first as
UV handedness and **wrongly pinned there** (the pier's faces were
mirrored *and* untangented, and both were changed at once). The wall
sweep proved otherwise: measured 320 tangents of which 160 were
mirrored, and adding the tangent stream alone fixed walls, stairs,
posts and slabs. **EVERY runtime mesh wearing the stone material must
call `RecalculateTangents()`** — the material does parallax occlusion
mapping, which ray-marches in TANGENT space, and a runtime mesh
carries no tangent stream unless asked. Not one mesh in this project
had them; it only surfaced when POM was switched on. Five sites fixed
(`WallEdge.BuildSectionMesh` — walls AND stairs, `WallNode`
BuildWedgeMesh ×2, `SlabTile.BuildPrism`, `Bridge.BuildMesh`,
`WallButtress.BuildPrism`).

**3. Two solid things that shouldn't have been.**
`CastleSite` markers (georeference handles, 6×30×6m boxes re-seated on
the terrain each Start) kept their colliders after their renderers were
hidden — invisible solids at every castle centre swallowing the wall
tools' first hit. They now disable their own colliders in `Awake`
(play mode only; edit mode keeps them selectable). Measured 11 of 12
stairs climbable with them solid, 12 of 12 inert — **I first reported
0 of 12 from a probe that happened to start on the one blocked flight,
and the user pushed back**; the corrected figure is in CLAUDE.md with
the lesson.

`WalkMode`'s capsule hangs below the lens by `CharacterController.center`,
which is a LOCAL offset — so pitching the camera swung the whole body
on a pendulum. Measured at 85° of look-up: 0.80m forward of the lens
with its feet 0.73m high. `PinBody` counters the rotation on the offset
(exact, since PhysX never tilts the capsule); verified live at
(0.00, −0.80, 0.00) at every pitch.

**4. The stair that would not climb.** The user: "not able to walk up
them anymore though I can jump and stand on them", later "only if the
camera is pointed nearly straight up", then "still not working".
Neither fix above was the cause. Every probe I wrote proved the climb
*worked* — including driving the user's REAL controller from their
real position, which climbed 5.62m in 0.67s. Simulation was exhausted,
so we **instrumented `WalkMode.Step`** with a per-frame trace (keys,
wish, distance asked vs achieved, grounded, collisionFlags, capsule
offset, flattest-normal contact) and the user made one attempt.

The trace named it immediately: 22 consecutive frames of `want 0.60
got 0.000`, aimed correctly, blocked on contacts with normal.y ≈ 0.42
— a tread's NOSE, not any face. `want` was 0.58m **per frame**:
`walkSpeed` 10 m/s at ~17 editor fps, into 0.249m treads — **2.3
treads in a single discrete sweep**. `CharacterController.Move` is one
sweep, so a move longer than the obstacle wedges on it. A/B on the
real flight: one Move per frame NEVER reached the top at 17 fps (187
stalled frames) and never left the first step at running speed;
capped, it reached the top at every speed and frame rate with zero
stalls. `WalkMode.MoveSwept` cuts each frame's travel into sweeps of
at most 0.15m — total displacement unchanged, so nothing about the
feel moves. The old 2.8 m/s default worked at 17 fps, which is why
"the same stairs used to work".

**5. Crenellations** (`WallCrenel.cs`, new). A battlement is a SPAN on
the edge (`WallEdge.Crenel` — two parameters plus the dialled height
and gap), never a per-edge flag: a chained run is many edges through
no fault of the player's. A merlon standing ON a joint is NODE data
(`WallNode.Crenel` — just a height), the clasping pier's rule again;
its seat and plan re-derive from the members every rebuild, which is
right where the pier's absolute top was right — a pier is founded in
the ground, a merlon sits on the wall and must follow it. The joint
block's plan is a RADIAL function of the members (each member's width
out to half a merlon, never less than the post radius), so a
straight-through node gives exactly a plain merlon (measured 1.00 ×
1.10m) and an L turns the corner (1.05 × 1.05m reaching along both) —
no case analysis anywhere. A split CLIPS a battlement rather than
dropping it, the deliberate difference from a doorway or a pier.
CastleSave v12.

The tool was reworked twice on the user's direction:
- Shipped as press-drag-release with a plain click meaning the whole
  wall. **Removed the same day**: "get rid of the full wall span and
  only use click and drag, just like placing a wall."
- The run now FOLLOWS THE MASONRY through the graph (`EdgePath`, the
  delete tool's own path finder) — a 35m drag came out as 7 walls, 8
  joints, 45 merlons in one gesture — standing back from each joint by
  `NodeReach + gap` so the block has its notch.
- Then three phases (the user's ask): HOVER rides a single real merlon
  on the cursor (or the joint's own block where it has snapped),
  DRAGGING lays out the run, releasing leaves it PENDING so the dials
  can be reached without holding the button, and a second click builds.
- Two dials, **persistent** across tool pickups: Ctrl+scroll merlon
  height (1.2m down to a quarter of it), Alt+scroll spacing (0.2–4m).
  Anchoring on an existing battlement adopts ITS height and spacing —
  on the same wall or through the node — so a run built in two drags
  reads as one piece of masonry.

**6. The lone-merlon centring fix** (found in the user's live session
at the very end). "The last crenel chain didn't place flush with the
ends of the walls it's placed on, even though it snapped to them."
Reading their actual world: the run's own endpoints WERE flush at
0.00m; the fault was the layout. A span holding one merlon left-aligned
it and dumped all its slack at the far end, so a short leg between two
joints gave one corner the dialled 0.80m embrasure and the other 1.30m.
A lone merlon is now centred — verified on their walls at 1.42m from
each end, with multi-merlon spans still landing on their ends at
0.000m.

## Trade-offs decided

- **Buttresses store no strength number** (the user's call): a pier
  will stiffen a wall when there is a siege to test it; inventing the
  figure before its consumer exists is guessing at a balance nothing
  can check.
- **The founding rule instead of a face restriction** (the user's
  call, after asking whether buttresses belonged anywhere but the
  outer face historically). Restrict the founding, not the placement.
- **Merlons are solid stone** (the user's call, asked explicitly with
  the measurement in hand: full-thickness merlons on 1.00m walls
  against a 0.70m capsule leave no lane). Settled afterwards by the
  user's clarification that **walls were never meant to be walkable** —
  a floor slab spanning two wall tops is the wall-walk, and that has
  been working well.
- **The merlon keeps its width and the embrasures absorb the fit** —
  scaling both makes every wall's blocks a slightly different size,
  which reads as sloppy stonework where two walls meet.
- **The crenel dials persist** rather than re-arming on tool pickup
  (the user's call): a parapet dialled once is the parapet this castle
  has.
- Demolition now refunds a wall's dressing when the edge dies WHOLE
  (fixing the same leak for buttresses); a partial delete carries the
  dressing onto the survivors, so refunding there would pay for stone
  still standing.

## Bugs hit

- The see-through buttress → the tangent rule (above). Diagnosed wrong
  first; the wall sweep corrected it.
- The founding clamp inverted (above); the user caught it.
- **Winding got mirrored TWICE on crenellations** — first all five
  merlon faces (the parapet was a hole seen from above and the cursor
  fell through it), then every side of the joint block. The tell both
  times was a raycast finding nothing. My own orientation test was too
  weak to catch the second one: it measured outwardness against the
  whole run's bounds centre, which is meaningless for a mesh of many
  separate blocks. Test per BLOCK.
- Two claims I made and had to retract: "0 of 12 stairs climbable"
  (actually 11 of 12 — probe started on the one blocked flight), and
  "your joint blocks were lost to my recompile" (all 15 were standing;
  `WallNode.All` reads EMPTY after a mid-play domain reload because
  `OnEnable` does not re-fire, so the probe was counting an empty
  registry). Both are now in CLAUDE.md as traps.

## Things tried that didn't stick

- Four rounds of probe-based theories about the stairs (CastleSite
  markers, the capsule pendulum, headroom, aiming) — all real fixes
  except the last, none of them the cause. **Reconstructing the input
  path in a probe cannot find a frame-rate bug, because a probe loop
  picks its own dt.** Instrumenting the real path found it in one
  attempt.
- The whole-wall crenel click (shipped and removed the same day).
- Rendering the battlement for a visual check: the scene is at night
  and HDRP's exposure defeated both a light boost and a brightening
  pass. Abandoned — the geometry is verified numerically instead.
