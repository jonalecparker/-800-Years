  2. *Swept meshes*: each piece generates a swept prism over its slice of the curve; neighbors share exact boundary parameters (watertight seams), arc-length U + world-anchored V texture coordinates (mortar lines flow across seams, courses align across bases, tall walls show more courses instead of stretching).

Crossing at ≥25° is a junction (masonry interpenetrates — the overlap hides inside the stone); near-parallel overlap blocks as a duplicate. Endpoint targeting uses the *same adjacency helpers as the grid tool* (point at a wall face or the cell beside it → the adjacent cell), sweeps extend half a cell past both endpoints so walls fill their endpoint cells, and each end probes backward along its own line — finding a wall within ~1.3m extends the sweep *into* it, sealing the joint at any approach angle. Endpoint-to-endpoint chaining snaps at 0.75m; Alt places free.

## Decisions & trade-offs

- **Spans over layers.** The variable-height ask exposed that the whole system was secretly voxel-shaped. Storing world-elevation spans per piece (rather than height multipliers) was chosen explicitly so terrain, courses, and future width rules all ride the same math. It paid off the same day, three times.
- **Stepped everything.** Heights (0.25×), curve bulge (0.5m), terrain bases (0.5m) are all quantized by design — repeatability/symmetry as a creative aid, matching the user's stated philosophy. Curves over the same chord are *reproducibly identical*, which is what makes stacked courses align.
- **The spline jump was deliberate and discussed.** Three grid-seam complaints in two days (jagged overlap → unequal widths → skinny slivers) each moved curved walls further off the cell model; rather than an equal-arc-on-grid refactor that would be half throwaway, the user chose ("big boss up") to promote curved walls to real spline objects. Straight walls and future buildings stay grid-native — the hybrid is the industry norm.
- **Occupancy via physics, not cells**, for spline walls — with an angle test making crossings junctions instead of conflicts. Grid keeps its cell occupancy; the two interoperate through overlap checks in both directions.
- **Wall connection = geometry extension, not endpoint relocation.** After trying flank-snapping (endpoint pulled onto the other wall's centerline), the user redefined the mechanism: endpoints stay cell-based, and the wall's own sweep extends backward into whatever stands behind it. Simpler mental model, honest ghost box.

## Bugs hit

- **Square footprints lie about direction, twice.** Grid pieces are 1×1 in plan, so their transform yaw says nothing about which way the *run* travels. This broke flank-axis snapping (endpoint landed on a piece's side edge) and then the junction angle test (perpendicular joins falsely blocked as "parallel"). Fix: pieces record `runYaw` at placement; all angle tests use it.
- **`ArcAt` clamps to [0,1]** — course sections over extended sweeps (t < 0 or > 1) computed wrong arc lengths; `ExtendedArcAt`/`TFromExtendedArc` extrapolate linearly at endpoint parametric speed.
- **Test-harness race**: `Destroy` defers to end of frame, so delete-then-rebuild in one `execute_code` call validated against still-alive walls, leaving "floating" pieces. `DestroyImmediate` in tests; not a tool bug.
- Neighbor-difference tangents quantized curve yaws to 45° steps (read as flat segments) — switched to the Bézier's analytic tangent.
- Two rounds of CS0165 (out-params after short-circuited `&&`) — pre-declare with defaults.
- HDRP motion blur smears screenshots taken the same frame as a camera teleport — always screenshot twice.
