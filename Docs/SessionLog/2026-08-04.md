# Session — 2026-08-04: stairs, a cutaway that cuts, and doors that know which floor they serve

*Covers 2026-08-03 → 08-04 — one long session across the date line.*

## Goal

The **stairs half** of the ⚠ storeys/stairs design call: design it, then build it. What followed was everything it took to make a stair actually *usable* — a way to see inside a roofed room, something to stand on at the top, a hole in the ceiling to arrive through, and a way in through the wall at the bottom.

Going in you could build a three-storey keep with no way to climb it. Coming out you can climb it, walk in the door, and see what you're doing while you build inside.

## What we did

- **`Docs/Stairs.md`** — the design brief, agreed before any code. A stair is the **first stored vertical relationship** in the model: everything else is planar and lives at exactly one `baseY`, and a stair belongs to two storeys at once. Rejected: a `WallEdge` variant (dies on the planar graph — it would need an `isStair` flag every query filters out, which is the infer-from-a-marker pattern the graph rebuild deleted) and a room (a ring is same-base by construction, and a forestair up a curtain wall has no enclosure at all).
- **`WallStair` / `WallStairStep`** — a rider on the graph the way `WallRoom` is: own `All` registry, registered in `OnEnable`, not a member of any `WallNode`, invisible to every planar query. Geometry is baked at commit and the host wall is **not stored** — the wall you pointed at is what the cursor resolver used, nothing more.
- **The run is a CHAIN of spans, not one curve** (found in playtest round one). Confined to a single `WallEdge` a stair pins itself to whichever node it started from, and a circle is eight edges. It now follows the masonry **through its nodes**, taking the straightest continuation on the same storey — sharing `StraightestContinuation` with the wall tool's ride.
- **One section per step.** Two `WallEdge` statics opened up (`SampleSliceGround`, and a new `BuildSpecFrame` split out of the old private `FinishSpec`). `BuildSectionMesh` needed **no change at all**: at a step boundary the taller section's start cap *is* the riser. New identity, borrowed geometry.
- **The gesture is no gesture.** Point at a wall's side face and the ghost is already there; the wall answers every question including how long the stair is. Because the length is derived there is **no pitch refusal** — the one refusal that survives is "not enough wall".
- **`WallStair.WeldOffsetJoints`** — offsetting each span independently doesn't leave them touching; at an octagon's corner the jump measured **0.84m**, three treads' worth. Welded at the miter, the same fix `WallRoom.InsetOutline` uses. Welding then changes the length (3.09m inside vs 4.91m outside for the same 3.5m climb), so the sizing loop rescales the walk until the finished run matches the climb — 1–4 passes, lands within about a degree.
- **`CutawayView` + `CutawayPivot`** — a horizontal plane through the world; masonry crossing it is **sliced**, masonry above it hidden and un-collidable. `PgUp`/`PgDn` step 0.5m, `Home` clears. Built because building *inside* a roofed room is impossible otherwise: the deck is in the way of the eye **and** is the first thing the cursor ray hits.
- **`WallStair.LandingRun`** (2.5m) — the run carried on level at `topY`, part of the same span chain because it follows the same masonry through the same nodes.
- **`WallRoom.wells`** — a stored `List<Well>` of `(owner, poly)`, cut at exactly two moments (`NotifyStairBuilt`, `TryBuildRoof`) and closed at one (`NotifyStairGone`). Both directions are needed because a roof over a finished stair and a stair under a finished roof are the same fact reached from opposite ends.
- **Doors** (`Docs/Doors.md`) — `WallEdge.Opening { t, width, head, sill }`, **stored on the edge, not a rider**: an opening is a hole in this masonry and nothing else's. A lintel is a section with a raised bottom, a threshold is one with a lowered top; the jambs are the neighbouring sections' end caps. **No new mesh code at all.** New `ToolMode.Door` and a Doors button in `BuildMenu`.
- **`Opening.sill`** — a doorway serves a *floor*, not the ground it stands on (`WallRoom.FloorServing`, `WallRoom.RaiseSills`).
- **Stairs climb to a doorway sill** — point the stair tool at a doorway's own stone and the run *arrives* at its sill rather than setting off from there (`WallStair.ReverseSpans`).
- **The section grid belongs to the wall, not to the doorway** (final fix, 08-04) — see "Bugs hit".

## Decisions & trade-offs

- **A stair is a rider, not a wall variant or a room.** It never splits a wall and no wall splits it; deleting the wall it leans on leaves it standing in the air, consistent with an upper storey over a deleted wall and with support being deferred.
- **No pitch refusal.** The pitch refusal first designed here was machinery that existed only to police a freedom worth not granting. Length is derived from rise; the player chooses where and which way, and nothing else.
- **The cutaway slices rather than hides, and does it with a transform scale.** Every mesh here is a vertical prism, so scaling Y about the object's own bottom walks the top face down and leaves everything else exact. View state stays out of geometry — no `WallEdge`, `WallNode` or `WallStair` code knows it exists, and nothing rebuilds because the cut moved. The collider rides along for free: **what you can see is what you can hit.**
- **The cutaway readout is centred at the top.** The BuildLog owns the left margin, and a view control that covers the log is a worse trade than the one it was solving.
- **Door width (1.1m) and head (2.1m) are fixed**, for the same reason the stair's width is: dialling them needs a fifth meaning for the scroll wheel, which has to join `ClaimingScrollWheel` arbitration.
- **Uphill entrance, honest doors** (user's call, 08-03). A room's floor is one level slab at its highest interior ground, so on a slope it stands above the downhill wall's footing and a doorway cut from that ground opened into the side of the slab. **The geometry is unavoidable**: a level floor over sloping ground steps on one side no matter what, and excavating to the lowest ground just moves the problem to the high side — the user caught that before I did. `max` also has a hidden second reason: footings quantize *down* and `floorY` quantizes *up*, so `floorY ≥ every wall's footing` is what stops daylight under the uphill walls. Kept the level floor, made the doors state their step.
- **Sill raising is one-way.** Deleting a room does not drop sills back down. A threshold left standing is visible and removable; a sill silently sinking into a slab would not be.
- **A sill needs three risers (0.66m) before the stair tool offers a flight.** Footings quantize down and floors up, so nearly every doorway has half a base step under it even on level ground. A doorstep stays a doorstep.
- **The well's hole is headroom, not footprint**, and is drawn 5cm narrower than the treads so the keyhole bridge has a strictly interior hole to work with.

## Dead ends

- **The pitch refusal** — designed in full, then cut in the same design session once "length is derived" made it meaningless.
- **All-or-nothing cutaway hiding.** Wrong twice over: every object vanishes at whatever height its own bottom sits at, so a room lost its roof, then its walls, then its corner posts in three unrelated steps — and the one view actually worth having (walls cut to knee height) existed at no level at all.
- **Excavating the room floor to the lowest ground** — considered and rejected on the user's argument that it only moves the step to the high side, where you'd then have to climb *down* into the room.
- **Offsetting each stair span independently**, and **sizing the run without correcting for the weld** — both would have quietly broken the very reason the tool needs no pitch refusal (±20% pitch drift).

## Bugs hit

- **Corner posts outlived the walls in the cutaway.** `WallNode.BuildWedgeMesh` starts a wedge 2cm below the walls it joins (`min(a.bottomY, b.bottomY) - 0.02f`), so posts were always the last thing standing. Fixed as a side effect of cutting everything at one plane.
- **`WorldSpan` read `Renderer.bounds`** — a renderer already sliced reports the *slice*, so the cut decided it had cleared the top of the world one step above wherever it sat and switched itself off. Spans now come from `CutawayPivot.authoredY` + live mesh bounds.
- **The ear clipper would have filled the well back in.** A bridged hole repeats its two seam vertices, and a repeat sits exactly ON the ear it duplicates, so the containment test blocked every ear near the seam and the cap fell to the `!clipped` fan fallback. `Triangulate` now skips coincident vertices.
- **A doorway in a boundary wall made the room lose its floor.** `BuildFloor` takes `max(bottomY)` to decide whether the walls stand on a deck; a lintel's bottom is 2.1m up, so one doorway made the room conclude it was an upper storey. It skips lintels now.
- **A stair anchored on a lintel began in mid-air** — `MasonryFloorAt` asks the nearest *solid* section on the same wall instead.
- **A threshold block grew taller than the wall it was in** (sill 48.00 in a wall topping out at 36.50, on a 15m-drop test patch). Clamped to the wall's own top, so the doorway is visibly walled up — with a log line naming the floor that did it — rather than becoming a hole into the middle of a slab.
- **Every doorway sprouted a stair offer**, including uphill ones, because of the quantization asymmetry above. Fixed with `DoorStepsNeeded`.
- **Interior walls with doors sank** (08-04, user report). Two causes, both in `WallEdge.BuildSpecs`, both the same mistake — letting the doorway decide something about the wall:
  - **A doorway re-divided the whole wall.** Solid runs were shared out evenly *after* the split, so cutting a hole anywhere moved every boundary — and a section's footing is the lowest ground under its footprint snapped down to the base step, so a longer section sits lower. A 4m partition divides into 4 sections of 1m; put a 1.1m door through the middle and each side becomes **one** section of 1.45m, which reliably drops half a base step. Half the wall sank. Long curtain walls went 1m → 1.09m and barely showed it, which is exactly why it read as an *interior wall* problem. The grid is now settled before any opening is cut into it, and a cell the doorway clips keeps its own footing.
  - **A doorway spanning a step took one figure for two jobs.** `ground` fed both the threshold's bottom and `wallTop = ground + height`, and that number is the *lower* one — so the masonry over the door sat half a step below the masonry beside it. A notch, every time, on the high side. It now reaches down to the lowest cell it covers and up to the highest.
- Deferred-`Destroy` traps, twice: a well test read the **old** roof because two `RoomRoof` objects existed in-frame (`BuildRoofSlab` now uses `DestroyImmediate`, matching `WallStair.Rebuild`, which also removes the stale-collider frame); and a `CreatePrimitive` probe cube flashed at the world origin for a frame (`SetActive(false)` before `Destroy`).

Self-inflicted, worth remembering:

- The first cutaway probe measured nothing because I used absolute cut levels of 2.0 and 1.0 — **the terrain there is at y≈53.5.**
- A `NullReferenceException` from reflecting `MasonryFloorAt` with instance `BindingFlags`; it is static.

## Open at end of session

- **Playtest round three** (from `Docs/Stairs.md`): the landing's feel at 2.5m, whether the well's headroom is generous enough to walk rather than clip, and the 5cm coplanar lip along the landing's outer edge. If that lip reads as a shimmer, the honest fix is a real polygon difference (the well as a notch in the outline), not a bigger lip.
- **A room built inside another room may lay a second floor slab coplanar with the first.** Seen in a probe on near-level ground: outer `floorY` 54.50, inner room also 54.50. `BuildFloor`'s elevated-room branch needs `wallBase > floorY + 0.25`, and a deck sitting exactly one base step above the terrain doesn't clear it. **Not confirmed** — I read both rooms' `floorY` but never checked whether a second slab object was actually built. On sloped ground the elevated branch fired correctly and no slab was made.
- **A doorway with between 0.6m and 2.2m of wall above its sill becomes a full-height gateway rather than a doorway** — `DoorRefusal` demands 0.6m, a lintel needs head + 0.1. Intentional for a low wall with the sill at grade; may read as damage where the sill has been *raised*.
