# Session — 2026-08-13/14: houses on the land, towns grown from the gate, and real ground under the castles

*One conversation (several compactions) spanning the 13th into the
14th. Three arcs: procedural village houses shipped on a stable lot
plan with a population dial to watch towns grow; the village LAND was
rebuilt from first principles as a gate-seeded flood region after the
user caught towns growing on one side of the road; and the castle-site
LIDAR went from "we should get this" to real 2m NRW ground baked into
the terrain, verified to centimetres.*

## Goal

Procedural houses on Living land (the 08-13 log's open item), then
whatever the user's eye caught — which turned out to be the town's
shape itself, the survey's load time, and the ground under Grosmont.

## What we did

**1. Village houses** (`VillageHouses.cs`, `Docs/VillageHouses.md`):
the STABLE LOT PLAN — `PlanLots` derives a deterministic ordered lot
list per town from stored facts (Living parcels + castle routes +
terrain), and population is just how many lots materialize. Houses are
a PREFIX: household #13 appears on the lot the plan always reserved,
nothing built ever moves (machine-verified 12→20: 42 kept, 0 moved, 8
appended; later 12→17→40→5 all exact). Church always (highest of the
first 8 lots), inn at 10 households, neighbor towns fixed at 12.
TimberWoods doctrine: regenerated every frame via `EnsureCurrent`
(two-int signature), never saved, root outside `splineParent`. The inn
got a distinct body after the user couldn't tell it from the houses
(jettied whitewashed upper storey over dark timber, painted sign at
the street corner), and **`[`/`]` dial households** (Shift ×5, build
mode only) so the user can watch the town grow.

**2. Lot-plan fixes from the user's flyover**: no lot within 10m of a
waterway's EDGE (houses stood in the carved Monnow); an 11-station
hole in Grosmont's street front diagnosed live — the parcel flood
traces boundaries ALONG the road stripe, so front lots fell 0.2–12.9m
outside the polygon — answered then by a 14m frontage tolerance; a
second row of lots with a 60m back-row penalty (the user's rule:
second-from-the-road beside the castle beats tenth-on-the-road).

**3. The village flood** (the session's centerpiece; the user's
first-principles call: "the town would have grown out from the
castle... an irregular circle"). The one-sided-town bug was
structural: village land was survey cells, and the parcel flood makes
cell boundaries lie ALONG roads, so a town almost never spanned its
own high street. Now the village is GROWN like the castle grounds are
fitted: a dedicated Dijkstra seeds where each trimmed approach leaves
the castle grounds (the gate; a roadless castle seeds the ring around
its grounds), road cost INVERTED (corridor ×0.35, not wall ×25),
water a punishing toll (×80 — a moated castle's hamlet escapes the
ring, a town never jumps its river otherwise), slope quadratic as
ever, 400m geometric leash, stopping at `villageAcres` (dial, 15).
The claim is a SECOND frozen mask in the main flood, so the town
spans its streets BY CONSTRUCTION; the traced polygon is ONE Living
parcel per lordship. The tier system, `BetweenRoadsBonus`,
`VillageMaxGrade` and the `villagePlots` dial are DELETED. Houses
fill the blob via back lanes (`OffsetPolyline` copies at 58m pitch —
burgage depth), and lots keep out of Castle parcels (`CastleClear`
8m) after houses leaked through the gate. Verified: all three towns
14.8–15.1 acres, Grosmont's town on BOTH sides at 26/28 road
stations, simple polygons, zero castle overlap, boundary vertices
shared with the castle parcel.

**4. Survey performance** (the user: "this should be instant... we
can compute this and store it permanently, right?"): the generated
survey now persists to `Assets/Terrain/SurveyCache-<BaseName>.json`,
keyed by an FNV signature of every input (dials, sites, a 33×33
heightmap sweep, `CacheVersion`); fresh sessions restore instead of
re-flooding; Resurvey invalidates first. The Survey button's
first-open stall (draping 5,600 overlays in one frame) is timesliced:
~6ms of rebuilds per frame via `LandPlot.Tick`, open measured ~68ms.

**5. The castle-site LIDAR** (`Docs/CastleLidar.md`, `CastleLidar.cs`):
found the NRW archive via DataMapWales (WFS tile catalogue → direct
blob URLs), downloaded 2m DTM for all four 10km squares the three
castles' patches touch (~265MB zips, extracted ~713MB ASC), and built
the importer the same session after the user hand-tested and found
the ground unchanged: an edit-mode bake (run after Split; Split is
the un-patch) that patches 600m around each site with a 150m feather,
fits a per-site datum offset over the feather ring, keeps out of
waterways (carved beds and ribbons survive), and reseats tree heights.
Georef: world → doc-anchored Web Mercator → WGS84 → Helmert → Airy
TM → BNG, verified against all three castles' real grid references
(~30–50m). Baked: 59,950 samples across 3 tiles; datum offsets
−23.85/−23.30/−23.49m (near-identity across 27k samples = the georef
proof); patch edge steps 0.07m over 10m; terrain matches lidar+offset
to centimetres on open ground. The survey cache invalidated itself
(signature sweeps the heightmap) and regenerated over the real
earthworks — by construction.

## Decisions & trade-offs

- **Village land grown, not picked** — the flood expresses the user's
  historical shape (blob at the gate, elongated along streets,
  stopped at the river) as a cost field instead of selection rules.
- **The world's datum wins, LIDAR provides shape**: the patch is
  offset-fitted to the base at the ring, not trusted absolutely.
- **2m now, 1m later**: the Wales 1m 2020-23 programme is point-cloud
  only so far; the WFS query hands us the upgrade when its rasters
  land.
- **The honest limit said out loud**: tiles sample at ~7.3m, so the
  2m data arrives DOWNSAMPLED — real spur/ditch/moat shapes, not 2m
  mesh density. The castle-tile resolution bump is phase 2 and next
  session's discussion.
- Old saves keep their cell-shaped Living parcels — stored facts stay
  stored; new shapes on fresh survey/Resurvey.

## Bugs hit

- **Trace fragmentation, three layers deep**: the village flood
  claimed 15.0 acres but White Castle's polygon traced 1.6 — 8-neighbor
  corridor claims string samples corner-to-corner and the outline
  tracer keeps one lobe of the figure-8 (→ 4-neighbor flood); then
  wrapping the rasterized castle grounds still kissed diagonally
  (→ pinch removal, costlier sample banned); then severing a corridor
  neck silently stranded the budget at 9.5 acres (→ pruning refunds
  acres into a re-flood round, plus a claimed-vs-traced warning so it
  can never fail silently again). Final: all towns at target.
- **Houses in the river / the skipped street stretch** — both
  diagnosed with live rejection tallies before touching code
  (the gap was InsideAny, not relief; the remaining "holes" after the
  fix turned out to be the riverbank lots correctly refused).
- Mono's `ZipArchive` refuses the NRW zips ("local file header is
  corrupt") — extracted with `Expand-Archive`, loader reads loose
  .asc.
- Eager ASC parsing would have text-parsed ~100M values for ground
  nothing uses — header-filter before data-parse.
- The MCP bridge dropped repeatedly during long main-thread bakes
  (known) — completion verified from console state, never re-run.
- First bake attempt refused correctly: the editor was still in play
  mode (the guard worked).
